## CPT

- Eligibility normally requires 2 full semesters (1 full academic year) as a full-time student in the US.
- Exception: graduate programs that require an internship in the student's first year are exempt from that 1-year rule.
- CPT requires a job offer before applying — cannot apply without one.
- No fee for CPT approval (may have separate internship-course tuition).
- 365+ days of full-time CPT makes a student ineligible for OPT afterward. (Stated as a day count, not "12 months.")
- Approval can take just a few days once all requirements are met, since it's approved by the school, not USCIS.

## OPT — general

- Eligibility: 1 full academic year (2 consecutive full-time terms) completed before requesting.
- OPT does not require a job offer to apply; employment is reported after the fact.
- Once pre-completion OPT is *approved*, that time is deducted from the 12-month total even if the student never works during it or doesn't use the full period.
- Total OPT cap: 12 months combined across pre- and post-completion, per degree level. Can only be used once per degree level (a second degree at the same level does not get a new allotment).
- Standard USCIS processing: 3–5 months. Premium processing: ~30 business days (~6 weeks), not counting mail time, at extra cost.
- New I-20 for OPT recommendation: up to 10 business days for the school to produce, before the student can even file with USCIS — a fixed delay to build into any timeline.
- Post-completion OPT: 90 unemployment days allowed total.
- Multiple jobs allowed on OPT simultaneously, provided combined hours ≥ 20/week and every job is related to the degree field — cannot mix one related and one unrelated job.

## STEM OPT extension

- 24-month extension after the first 12 months of OPT.
- Requires an employer enrolled in E-Verify (a general federal requirement — applies to any employer, not school-specific).
- Requires a signed Form I-983 training plan with the employer.
- Must be a paid position, minimum 20 hrs/week, related to the STEM field.
- Self-employment is not allowed on STEM OPT (allowed on regular OPT, not on the extension).
- Can file up to 90 days before the current EAD end date, must file before that end date.
- If filed on time, the student may keep working up to 180 days past the EAD end date even without the new card yet in hand.

## Cap-gap

- Triggered when a student is selected in the H-1B lottery and OPT would otherwise expire before the H-1B start date (Oct 1).
- Extension is described only as "continue working on OPT up until your H-1B status change" — **no specific end date (Sept 30 vs. April 1) was stated in the session.** Still needs independent verification against the current USCIS cap-gap page.
- Student must request a Cap-Gap I-20 from the international office once the H-1B change-of-status filing is in.

## SSN timing (resolves prior ambiguity)

- Can apply up to 30 days before the start date on the I-20 (for CPT/academic training).
- Can apply up to 30 days before the start date on the EAD card (for OPT).
- The SSN itself is permanent — obtained once, used for life, never reapplied for at a new work-authorization stage.

## Employment authorization — general principles

- Any compensation in any form (cash, housing, meals, gifts) in exchange for work counts as employment requiring authorization.
- Genuine volunteering (per Dept. of Labor definition) requires: no compensation or expectation of it, not displacing a paid role, and must be for a nonprofit — not a for-profit company even unpaid.
- Work physically performed outside the US does not require US work authorization, regardless of F-1 status, but the home country's own work-authorization rules and the student's academic enrollment obligations still apply, and the student must not resume any unauthorized work upon returning to the US.
- On-campus employment: max 20 hrs/week combined across all on-campus roles during fall/spring; authorization is tied to the degree program and ends at I-20 expiry or program completion, whichever is earlier.

## Founding a startup as an F-1 student

- Market research and early-stage exploration may be possible without work authorization.
- Once a student takes concrete action to advance a venture — registering the business, raising funds, pitching to investors — work authorization is required at that point, same as any other employment.
- Equity/share-holding has separate legal implications; recommended to discuss with legal counsel.
- Startup involvement (titles, activity) is subject to review by USCIS in future visa/OPT applications, so public profiles (LinkedIn, resume) should be accurate and dated correctly.

## Taxes

- All international students must file US tax forms annually, regardless of whether they worked. Deadline: April 15.
